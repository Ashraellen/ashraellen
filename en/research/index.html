Ashraellen

Entry | Public | Books

Research

Ashraellen is not merely a name, but a mode of seeing.

It is a mode of consciousness in which the world is perceived not as a collection of separate events, thoughts and reactions, but as a web of meanings, relations, knots and reflections. In this mode, one meaning opens another: the personal connects with the social, the everyday with the symbolic, the spiritual with language, fear with belief, pain with worldview, and a casual phrase may reveal an entire layer of human self-deception.

Ashraellen is a lifelong artistic-philosophical inquiry into meaning.

It does not arise from the desire to produce texts, books, videos or media formats, but from a specific mode of consciousness: the ability to perceive meanings inside meanings, structures within structures, and intersections where, on the surface, there may seem to be only a thought, a casual phrase, an everyday scene or a personal reaction.

Within this inquiry, the author is present not as the creator of a final system and not as the owner of truth, but as an observer and recorder of intersections. The task is to see the structure of a knot and record it before it dissolves again into the noise of habitual perception.

Each insight here does not close the inquiry, but opens another level: higher, deeper, lower, wider. Within what has already been understood, something not yet understood begins to appear. For this reason, the work cannot end with one book, one video, one thesis or one formula. Each form records one cross-section of what has been seen, while the next layer is already beginning to emerge behind it.

Books, novel cycles, satirical forms, symbolic analyses, videos, sound works, public formulas, comments and the multilingual archive are not separate content products. They are different ways of recording one long investigation.

At the centre of Ashraellen is not content production, but the observation of how a human being loses contact with reality, meaning, inner freedom and living Truth; how interpretation replaces direct perception; how the ego builds defences; how thought becomes a cage; how pain turns into worldview; and how language, system, ideology, religion, the digital environment or personal trauma begin to think in place of the human being.

Literary form is not decoration for an idea here. It becomes a way of knowing. A novel allows the mechanism to be lived from within. Satire reveals self-deception without moralising. A short formula records the moment of recognition. Video and sound carry rhythm, pressure and state. The website preserves traces of the inquiry outside the disappearing flow of social media.

Ashraellen does not offer a ready-made system of rescue, motivation or spiritual instruction. It creates forms of observation through which a person may recognise mechanisms already active within themselves. What a person does with what has been seen remains their own responsibility.

What is being researched

Not a separate anxiety.

Not a separate dependency.

Not a separate error of perception.

But the whole mechanism: how a person loses inner ground, begins to mistake reaction for event, lives inside other people’s scripts, and functions where presence should have been.

Information noise, the power of language and interpretation, the digital environment as a substitute for living perception, fear of the future, loss of meaning and existential burnout are not a list of symptoms.

They are a map of the territory.

Ashraellen investigates not the human being as an object, but the way a person ceases to see themselves, reality and their own participation in what is happening.

How it is done

Novels work as research instruments.

Short texts and videos act as fast sensors.

Formulas record the point of recognition.

Satire reveals self-deception without turning into a lecture.

Sound and visual images carry a state where direct explanation would already be too crude.

Social networks are the field layer of the inquiry: a thought meets live response, resistance, irritation, gratitude and sudden recognition.

Fragmented form, continuous inquiry

Contemporary digital culture rarely allows a person to enter a complex system sequentially, from the first chapter to the last. Meaning is increasingly assembled through fragments: a short text, a video, an image, a sound, a scene, a comment, or a return to the same thought much later.

Ashraellen explores how sustained philosophical inquiry can exist in such an environment without losing conceptual depth, internal coherence or literary identity.

The task is not to simplify thought for accelerated perception, but to change its form without draining its substance. A book preserves duration and internal development. A short text records a knot of meaning. Video joins thought with voice, rhythm and image. Sound carries a state. Separate fragments become different entrances into one continuous field of inquiry.

The question is not how to shorten a complex thought, but whether fragmented digital perception can become a carrier of a coherent philosophical system.

Method

The method of this work is observation.

Not an external system, not an academic procedure and not a set of ready-made theses, but an attentive discernment of how explanations, reactions, impulses and attempts to name what is happening arise within consciousness.

For more on why artistic form, first-person observation and the reader’s response become part of the research, see “Method of Observation”.

How to understand this work

Ashraellen can be approached from several directions at once.

For a reader, it is a place where inner mechanisms become visible.

For a cultural institution, it is a long-term artistic research practice.

For the digital age, it is an independent archive of human attention, exhaustion, meaning and discernment.

For a research context, it is a form of practical observation in which literature, public response, language, image and the digital environment are not external presentation, but part of the research itself.

Artistic research

The work uses novels, essays, video, sound, images and public response as instruments of observation. Literature here is not decoration for an idea, but a way of researching how inner mechanisms are lived from within.

Open knowledge

The project makes philosophical reflection accessible outside closed academic or institutional spaces. Its multilingual structure allows the same field of observation to be approached across different languages and cultural contexts.

Digital society

Ashraellen observes how digital speed, information noise, algorithmic pressure and fragmented attention affect meaning, identity, inner ground and the ability to remain in contact with reality.

Independent digital archive

The website functions as a structured archive of texts, books, observations, public fragments and research directions. It preserves the work outside the flow of social media, where meaning disappears too quickly to be seen.
