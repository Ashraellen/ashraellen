Ashraellen

Ashraellen’s Position

Ashraellen works as an independent artistic researcher. His field of observation is inner human experience: how a person perceives what is happening, creates explanations, mistakes an interpretation for Reality itself, and loses contact with Reality, meaning, inner freedom and their own presence.

Literature, philosophy, religion, language, image, sound and public response are used in this work not as proofs of a finished system, but as modes of inquiry. Religious and philosophical teachings, together with accounts of transcendent experience, are treated as models of the human encounter with Reality — important and sometimes profound, yet not identical with Reality in all its fullness.

This position is not offered to the reader as a compulsory doctrine. It is the worldview and research position from which Ashraellen observes, writes and acts.

Reality: acceptance is not approval

In his personal metaphysical position, Ashraellen proceeds from the existence of the Creator and from the understanding that neither existing Reality nor anything that has truly happened stands outside the Creator and His design.

Acceptance arises from this premise. For Ashraellen, it is not merely a psychological way of reducing inner resistance. Once an event has happened and become part of Reality, calling it a departure from the Creator’s design would require separating the Creator from existing Reality into two independent domains.

Denying what happened cannot make it unhappen. Acceptance begins with an acknowledgement: this exists; this happened; this is now what must be faced.

But acceptance is not approval.

Violence does not cease to be violence. A lie does not become truth. Cruelty, coercion and destruction do not receive moral justification merely because they occurred. A person retains the capacity to distinguish, judge, choose and answer for their own participation.

Ashraellen may reject a particular way of acting for himself, refuse to continue it and name the harm it caused directly. His moral judgement of what happened may remain negative. But that judgement does not become a claim that the very existence of the event was an error in Reality or a departure from the Creator’s design.

To accept a fact is not to call it good. To recognise metaphysically that something occurred is not to justify morally what was done.

Action without war against Reality

Acceptance does not demand passivity or oblige a person to preserve the existing state of affairs.

Within this position, resisting violence, protecting oneself and others, ending destructive relationships, refusing to take part in coercion and changing circumstances do not contradict acceptance of what has happened.

Changing what is happening is itself part of what is happening. Resistance, when it arises, does not fall outside Reality or oppose it from somewhere beyond.

The distinction lies not between action and inaction, but between action and an inner war against the fact of existence itself.

Decisive action does not require the belief that the universe made a mistake which a person must now correct in place of the Creator. Protecting life does not require declaring oneself the supreme judge of the whole design. A person can say “no” to what is happening through their own choice without demanding that Reality retroactively revoke its “this happened.”

Action is not an inner war against existence.

Another person’s path

Ashraellen proceeds from the understanding that genuine insight cannot be produced by coercion.

External pressure can make a person repeat words, obey rules, display loyalty, fear punishment or perform agreement. It cannot create the inner seeing to which that person has not yet arrived by their own path.

No one can live another person’s life in their place. No one can pass through their mistakes, consequences, discoveries and moments of recognition beforehand. No one can reliably assign a deadline by which they must understand what the speaker already understands.

Ashraellen sees his possibility elsewhere: to speak, to show, to leave a book, a thought, an image, a sign or a direction; to remove an obstacle or open a possibility; to answer when asked.

The transmission of understanding ends where the appropriation of another consciousness begins. A demand for immediate acceptance does not accelerate an inner path. The speaker’s persistence does not make another person’s understanding their property or achievement.

Another person’s path belongs to the one who walks it.

Religions and historical systems

Ashraellen does not regard the historical existence of religions, philosophical teachings, states, ideologies and other systems as an error in Reality, an error of history, or something that should never have existed at all.

This does not deny concrete errors within those systems. Particular ideas, doctrines, decisions, actions and practices may be mistaken, false, cruel or destructive. They can be named as such and judged negatively in moral terms.

Religions and historical systems arose in particular circumstances and performed historical functions. They could give people meaning, hope, community, discipline, a language for speaking about the transcendent and a way to endure what might otherwise have been unbearable.

At the same time, those same systems could become sources of fear, persecution, coercion, forced conversion, suppression and power over human consciousness.

Recognising a constructive side does not require denying a destructive one. Recognising a destructive side does not turn the whole historical phenomenon into a meaningless error. To acknowledge a historical role is not to justify morally every one of its manifestations.

Understanding the role of the past also creates no obligation to repeat its methods.

The fact that coercion existed and shaped history does not mean that Ashraellen accepts its continuation in his own work. His position is not to pass a final sentence on the past, but to refuse to reproduce its violent methods.

A refusal of messianism

Ashraellen refuses messianism not because he denies truth, spiritual knowledge, the Creator or the possibility of profound understanding.

He refuses to appropriate the right to save another person by force.

The boundary lies between transmitting one’s own understanding and demanding compulsory conversion. Between bearing witness and applying pressure. Between offering something to be heard and pursuing the person who did not hear it.

When a person begins to shame, intimidate, coerce, demand submission and decide what another must understand and by what date, a spiritual or philosophical idea ceases to be testimony and becomes an instrument of power.

Another person’s understanding does not belong to the speaker.

For Ashraellen, refusing messianism means being able to say honestly what he considers necessary, without concealing his position and without declaring himself the owner of the listener’s consciousness.

The human being as a radio point

To describe his role, Ashraellen uses the metaphor of a radio point.

A radio point does not create the whole signal and does not own the entire radio network. It becomes the place through which a particular message sounds at a particular moment.

Ashraellen does not regard himself as the owner of absolute truth, an infallible prophet or the Creator’s exclusive voice. He sees himself as one of countless radio points. Every person is a radio point of their own.

Each person carries and transmits what they have been, in a sense, “entrusted to carry” — that is, what at that moment they can receive and conduct through their own understanding, experience and structure of consciousness.

The difference between people does not mean that some radio points are “higher” and others “lower.” Their construction, experience, function, circumstances and present range are different.

Through one radio point comes a warning, through another doubt, through a third consolation; through others may come art, resistance or a new way of seeing.

A destructive idea or an error can also pass through a person. Error becomes part of the wider process, but it does not thereby become truth.

A radio point cannot simply be switched to what its present construction cannot receive and transmit. If a person has no inner range for a certain understanding now, pressure does not create that range. It may produce submission, imitation, repetition of someone else’s words, fear or performed agreement — but not genuine inner acceptance.

The difference between people is therefore not a network error. It arises from differences among the points of transmission themselves.

A person’s construction need not remain unchanged. Their range may widen or alter through experience, pain, love, mistakes, consequences, learning, observation and inner maturation.

A radio point can change. But it cannot be forcibly switched from outside to a signal it is not yet able to conduct.

In this sense, the metaphor is deliberately imperfect. Historically, a wired radio point carried a centralised broadcast and usually gave the listener no freedom to choose the frequency. For Ashraellen, this imperfection only deepens the meaning: a person, too, does not choose all their inner material at every moment from an infinite menu.

A person already exists within language, culture, body, era, family history, lived experience, trauma, limitations and attained understanding. They transmit what they can conduct through themselves now, and can easily mistake the signal passing through them for the only possible signal.

Yet no individual radio point is the whole radio network. It receives no exclusive right to speak for the Source and cannot declare other points mistaken merely because something different sounds through them.

Ashraellen’s task is to voice honestly what has passed through his experience and understanding. Whoever can hear it now will hear it. Whoever rejects it, does not hear it or understands it differently is walking their own part of the path.

A radio point is not responsible for the condition of every receiver, but it is responsible for the integrity of its own transmission.

Responsibility

The image of the radio point does not release Ashraellen from responsibility for his own words and actions.

He may not be the source of the whole signal, but he is responsible for how he conducts it through himself: for distortion, lies, manipulation, pressure, the use of fear, the transformation of a spiritual idea into a means of power, and the consequences of his own actions.

He is responsible for what he says; for how honestly he transmits his understanding; for whether he substitutes a desire for profit, influence or approval for what he has seen; and for whether he uses another person’s vulnerability to control them.

He is responsible for whether his own life corresponds to what he says.

He is responsible for being willing to notice his own errors, limitations and distortions. His present understanding is not declared final: it may expand, become more precise and change.

But responsibility for one’s own transmission does not become an obligation to secure another person’s agreement.

The listener’s decision — to accept, reject, forget, rethink, or return to what was said many years later — remains part of the listener’s path.

The radio point is responsible for the integrity of its own transmission, but does not appropriate control over every receiver.

Inquiry without a final point

Ashraellen conducts this inquiry first of all because he wants to understand for himself. A book, a text, a conversation or another work records not a final answer, but the understanding that has been reached at a particular moment.

Here, the very idea of a final answer loses its meaning. Every answer that is found changes the position from which the next question arises. Today’s understanding may therefore expand tomorrow, change, or prove insufficient to such a degree that it has to be abandoned.

That does not turn the earlier understanding into a lie retroactively. If it was formulated honestly, it remains an accurate record of the point the inquiry had reached at that time.

For the same reason, Ashraellen does not treat religious, philosophical, artistic, systems-based and scientific languages in advance as impermeable domains. At times, different languages make it possible to approach structurally similar questions from different sides.

But similarity of the question does not mean equality of evidence. A scientific claim requires scientific evidence. A philosophical model remains a philosophical model. Religious understanding remains religious understanding. One language may help something become visible through another, but it does not automatically acquire the other language’s standards of proof.

For Ashraellen, the value of such an encounter lies not in reducing everything to a single formula, but in the possibility that a new point of understanding may arise between different ways of describing — and, with it, the next question.

Not a final system

Ashraellen’s position is not a religious sermon, a missionary programme or a declaration of objective scientific truth.

It does not claim philosophical first discovery. The distinctions between acceptance and approval, action and inner resistance, testimony and coercion appeared in human thought long before Ashraellen. Here they are formulated as the working worldview position of his own inquiry.

This position does not justify passivity, violence, lies or moral indifference. It does not demand the abandonment of resistance, protection or moral choice.

It is a public statement of the point from which the inquiry proceeds: recognition of Reality without compulsory approval of every event; action without an inner war against existence; transmission of understanding without appropriating another person’s path; responsibility without messianism.

Ashraellen does not ask the reader to believe in his infallibility.

He offers these words to be heard, leaving the reader to decide whether they bear upon their own experience.

My part is to speak honestly.

The signal has sounded.

What follows belongs to the Creator.
