Public Formula 02

The Same Forces, New Names

The gods and demons of modern people have not disappeared. They have only received new names.

Meaning

Modern anxieties, addictions, and neuroses are viewed as new names for ancient gods and demons. A person may change the language without becoming free from service.

Full text

Modern people pay for their faith with one strange price: an almost total absence of self-observation. They consider themselves rational, efficient, and fully in control of their own lives, yet remain possessed by forces beyond their control. Their demons and gods, identical in essence, have not disappeared. They have merely received new names. Now a person lives in anxiety, vague inner unrest, psychological complications, an insatiable pull toward medication, alcohol, tobacco, food, and the great mass of neuroses that people have long learned to call simply life. They still serve the same forces. They simply do it without candles, without an altar, and with good internet — unless even that is forbidden.

Why it was chosen

This formula shows the continuity between ancient forms of inner service and modern psychological language.

Research note

The change of terms does not always mean liberation. Sometimes it only hides the old altar under a new vocabulary.
