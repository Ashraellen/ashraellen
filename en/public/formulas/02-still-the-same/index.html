Public Formula 02

The Same Forces, New Names

The gods and demons of modern people have not disappeared. They have only received new names.

Meaning

Modern anxieties, addictions, neuroses and compulsions are viewed here as new names for ancient forces. A person may change vocabulary without becoming free from service.

The formula is not a return to superstition. It is an attempt to notice that the inner mechanisms once described through gods and demons still move through people, only now with smoother language and better packaging.

Full text

Modern people pay for their faith with one strange price: an almost total absence of self-observation.

They consider themselves rational, efficient, informed and fully in control of their own lives. They explain themselves with psychology, productivity, diagnostics, lifestyle, stress, trauma, chemistry, circumstances and childhood. All of this may be useful. But useful language does not automatically make a person free.

The forces themselves do not disappear because the dictionary has changed.

Their demons and gods, identical in essence, have not vanished. They have merely received new names.

What was once called possession may now be called addiction.

What was once called temptation may now be called an irresistible impulse.

What was once called service to an idol may now be called career necessity, social expectation, image management or personal branding.

What was once called fear of death may now politely appear as anxiety, control, planning, insurance, ideology or the endless maintenance of a correct life.

The altar has not disappeared. It has simply become more ergonomic.

A person lives in anxiety, vague inner unrest, psychological complications, an insatiable pull toward medication, alcohol, tobacco, food, screens, approval, outrage, status and the great mass of neuroses that people have long learned to call simply life.

And still the person says: I am free.

Of course, добрый человек. Entirely free. Only do not take away the phone, the attention, the substance, the ritual, the enemy, the opinion, the fear, the hope and the carefully polished explanation of why all this is absolutely necessary.

The ancient world at least knew that a person can serve something invisible.

The modern world often serves the same things with a charger in the wall and a calendar full of respectable reasons.

People still serve the same forces.

They simply do it without candles, without an altar, and with good internet — unless even that is forbidden.

Why it was chosen

This formula was chosen because it connects ancient symbolic language with modern psychological and social mechanisms. It does not claim that old words should replace new ones. It asks a sharper question: has the person become freer, or only renamed the cage?

The public value of the formula lies in its diagnostic simplicity. It catches the moment where modern vocabulary begins to hide an old form of dependence.

Research note

The change of terms does not always mean liberation. Sometimes it only hides the old altar under a new vocabulary. For Ashraellen, this is part of a wider investigation into ego, fear, service, dependency and the structures that people obey while believing they are acting independently.

The formula works especially well in public form because it exposes the continuity beneath fashionable language. The costume changes. The mechanism remains.
