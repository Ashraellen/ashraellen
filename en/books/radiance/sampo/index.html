Chapter Three. Hunk

The morning after the first impossible night: bread, land, firewood and a human attempt to understand where the supply ends and participation begins.

Open chapterClose chapter

In the morning the bread lay on the table as if nothing had happened during the night.

It was almost cheeky.

Sofia stopped in the kitchen doorway and looked at the loaf for a few seconds without entering. The house had already woken up, although no one announced the rise. Footsteps creaked along the stairs, water flowed angrily through the pipes, a door slammed somewhere, someone coughed, someone too carefully put a cup on the table. The night passed, but did not clean up after itself.

Bread lay in the middle of a long wooden table.

Dark crust, uneven side, trace of yesterday's knife, a few crumbs on the board. Nearby there is oil in a small ceramic bowl, salt, a knife with a dark handle, berries in a wide bowl, mushrooms, already fried and smelling of the forest so directly, as if the forest did not agree to stay outside.

Everything was normal.

And that is why Sofia was not able to enter right away.

There was still a thin dark strip of soil under the thumbnail.

She washed her hands twice. First in the evening, before going to bed. Then in the morning, when I woke up before Eve and stood for a long time at the small sink under the sloping ceiling. The water was cold, the soap smelled of something pine, the towel scratched the skin. The earth has almost disappeared. Almost. But a thin line remained where the nail turned into the finger.

Sofia could clean it with a brush.

She didn't clean it up.

Not out of respect. Not out of mystical awe. She just couldn't decide right away whether she wanted to erase the evidence or maintain the possibility of doubting that it was evidence.

“Good morning,” Eve said behind her.

Sofia shuddered.

- Do you always walk like this?

- How?

- Inaudible.

- No. Only when a person stands in the kitchen doorway and looks at the bread as if it were a suspect.

Sofia wanted to answer, but remained silent.

Eve walked by, took two cups, and put them on the table. Her hair was tied up casually, and an old woolen sweater lay on her shoulders. She looked like a person who slept poorly, but had long ago made a truce with bad sleep.

- Tea? she asked.

- Is there coffee?

- Yes. But Marek says that the coffee here does not invigorate, but reminds the body of previous mistakes.

— Then tea.

- Reasonable.

Lina was already sitting on the bench by the window. She was peeling a small apple with a knife. The peel was removed with a long thin strip, almost without tearing. Lina had the hands of a person who does not make unnecessary movements.

Nora stood by the stove and looked into the fire. Didn't warm up. I was just watching.

David entered after Sophia, already dressed, with wet hair and the expression on his face that happens to people who decide to start the day with order and discover that order woke up before them and went about its business.

“The water in the shower changes from hot to ice without warning,” he said.

Marek, who was sitting at the edge of the table with a notepad, did not raise his head.

— The warning is on the water itself.

- Where?

- In her character.

Lina smiled without taking her eyes off the apple.

Sofia sat closer to the window. From here you could see the lake between the trees. The morning was gray, humid, not fully awakened. The sky remained low, as if during the night the house had risen a little higher than it should have, and now its roof rested on the clouds. The grass near the porch was dark with dew. Behind the barn stood birch trees, and the white trunks seemed not light, but washed by the cold.

Nick was not there.

This bothered me for some reason.

Then he entered.

I entered as people enter, ready in advance to prove that the day has not broken them. The hair is still damp, the shirt is buttoned crookedly at the collar, the face is cheerful with that dangerous cheerfulness that rests on the internal command “not to think.”

“Good morning to all participants...” he began and faltered.

Marek looked up.

- That's better.

- I haven't said anything yet.

- That's it.

Nick sat next to Sophia, but not too close. Yesterday, by the fire, he sat shoulder to her shoulder, as if ordinary proximity could protect against the unusual. In the morning he kept a short distance.

- Did you sleep? - he asked quietly.

- Almost.

- Me too. The ceiling turned out to be a medium-heavy interlocutor.

- And what did he say?

— Mostly creaked. But with an attitude.

Sofia looked at his hands.

There was nothing on the fingers. No soil, no crumbs, no traces of the night. He noticed her gaze and placed his palms on the table too calmly.

- What?

- Nothing.

- This word is now under our surveillance.

She turned to the window.

Thomas was the last one to enter, heading straight to the table. His glasses sat a little lower than usual, and there were shadows under his eyes. He looked like a man who had spent the night not in fear, but in calculation.

- Has anyone seen Ivar? - Thomas asked.

“We saw it,” said Marek.

- Where?

- Here.

- Now?

- No.

Thomas stood there for a few seconds.

— I asked to find out.

— I answered so as not to lie.

Nick took the cup, looked into it, found that it was empty, and put it back.

— Morning shows signs of life.

Ivar appeared through the side door.

Didn't log in as owner. He didn’t come in as the one they were waiting for. He simply appeared with an armful of thin logs, as if the house had asked, and he heard before the others. He was wearing a dark sweater and had his hair in a long braid over his shoulder. The silver in it in the morning light was not old age, but the cold shine of water. He placed the firewood by the stove, selected two logs, opened the door and placed them so that the fire would not flare up suddenly, but would accept the new fuel calmly.

Nick looked at Sophia.

Very quickly.

Ivar said nothing.

He ​​closed the stove, took an empty kettle, poured water from the jug and put it on the stove.

That's it.

No symbol.

And from this the kitchen itself began to search for what it was.

Lina placed the apple peel on the edge of the plate.

- Cutting bread? she asked.

The question was ordinary. Necessary. Morning.

Nick reached for the knife too quickly.

- I can.

Lina looked at his hand.

- You can.

She didn't give him the knife.

She simply removed her hand.

Nick took the knife.

And he began to cut the bread.

Sofia immediately realized that he did not cut bread.

He ​​sets a boundary.

The first slice came out thin. Too thin. Almost transparent at the edge. The second one is even thinner. The knife moved slowly, evenly, with almost legal precision. Nick was in no hurry, didn't joke, didn't look around. The crust gave in reluctantly, the crumb compressed under the blade, but he still drew a line so precisely, as if it depended on this line whether the world could become measurable again.

“If you continue,” Marek said, “in ten minutes we’ll be able to look through breakfast.”

Nick stopped.

— I respect the resource.

- You are interrogating him.

Lina quietly put the butter on the table.

Thomas took one of the thin slices, looked at it as if the bread had failed him as a form of matter, and reached for a knife.

- Is it possible?

Nick gave him the knife.

Thomas cut himself a piece.

Thick.

Not rude. Not greedy in the usual sense. It's just too reliable. The slice turned out strong, dense, with a large crust and a heavy side. He lay down on the plate with a sound like he was taking a position.

Thomas heard it himself.

He ​​looked at the slice, then at the loaf, then at the knife in his hand. His movement froze where a person can still pretend that everything is normal.

Nick didn't say a word.

Ivar stood at the stove and poured boiling water into a large kettle. Didn't look at Thomas. Or he looked differently than with his eyes.

“You can take it,” he said.

The kitchen became quieter.

The kettle continued to ring subtly with hot water.

Thomas looked at his hunk.

The thin strip of soil under the nail seemed darker.

The handle was warm from someone else's hands.

There was a chain in the warmth: Lina, Nick, Thomas, now she. Each one held the same object and through it, without meaning to, communicated something of their own to the table.

Sofia placed the blade against the crust.

The bread resisted.

Not much. How good bread should resist. The crust held its shape, the crumb underneath was dense, moist, and alive. The knife entered with a quiet crunch.

She cut off the middle slice.

Not thin.

Not thick.

And then I was angry with myself for noticing.

Nick noticed too.

“Compromise hunk,” he said almost without a voice.

- Be quiet.

- Yes, that's better.

He ​​actually fell silent.

And it was more useful than a joke.

At this point, Thomas placed his thick piece on the edge of the plate and did not eat it.

He ​​took the berries.

Then mushrooms.

Then tea.

The chunk remained.

Not forgotten. Too visible to be forgotten. It lay next to the plate, like something taken for future use and suddenly losing the right to be innocent.

Sofia saw that Ivar had noticed too.

He ​​didn't say anything.

Breakfast continued.

People gradually began to speak. Not about the night. About the water in the shower, about the weather, about whose socks are drying, about the fact that communication can only be made on the top step of the porch and only if you hold the phone like a sacrificial dish. Normal speech returned cautiously to the house.

“I went out onto the porch this morning,” said David, “and the phone showed three bars.”

- And? - Nick asked.

- I raised my hand - they disappeared.

— The phone does not like gestures from above.

- I sat down and appeared.

- So the phone respects fatigue.

- Very scientific.

— I try to match the level of communication.

Lina laughed quietly.

The laughter passed easily. He did not destroy the tension, but allowed it not to become a pose.

Sofia ate her slice slowly.

The bread was delicious.

If it was strange, bitter, too warm, too earthy, you might get scared and put the plate down. But it was simply delicious. Dark, dense, slightly sour, with a subtle sweetness from the grain and a dry crunch from the rind. Bread did what bread was supposed to do: feed.

And that’s why everything became more difficult.

Because last night the man in the snow also wanted to be fed.

He ​​didn't want to become a villain.

He ​​wanted the child not to cough on the bench, so that the bag was not too light, so that the wife at the stove did not silently count how much was left. He wanted stability. I wanted warmth. I wanted to not depend on the neighbor's door.

Sofia saw his hand on the warm snow.

And yours - on bread.

There was a difference.

Should have been.

But it was no longer so comfortable.

- Sofia? - asked Eva.

- What?

- You are far away.

- Not really.

- Okay.

Eve did not return it further.

Ivar, meanwhile, took the empty basket from the door and left.

No announcement. Without “after breakfast we will do this.” He simply left, like a person for whom the morning consists not of meanings, but of deeds.

Marek stood up next.

— We need wood at the stove. Who is not afraid of the outside world?

Nick raised his hand.

- I'm afraid, but culturally.

- This is not a qualification.

- Then I will spiritually support the others from a safe distance.

Sofia realized that the question was not about firewood.

- I'll go.

“I’m with you,” Nick said.

“No,” she said faster than she had intended.

Nick fell silent.

She saw that she had hurt him and immediately regretted it.

- Take gloves.

- So that later there will be no heroic epic about a splinter.

Sofia took the gloves.

- By hand.

She looked at him.

He ​​held his gaze calmly.

She came out.

The morning air greeted her not even with cold, but with humidity. It was full of water, but not rain. The water stayed on the grass, on the porch railing, on the stones along the path, on the gray bark of an old birch tree. It was easy to breathe and at the same time difficult, as if each breath brought inside not only air, but also part of the wet world.

The house behind me made a muffled noise. Voices, dishes, steps. Ahead stood a barn, low, dark, with a door that hung slightly crooked and therefore looked more honest than most doors.

Sofia walked towards him along a narrow path.

The grass wet my boots almost immediately.

Last night she would have noted this as a domestic inconvenience. Today the wet grass touched her feet precisely, coldly, without anger. Just a reminder: everything concerns here. Nothing remains a pure picture.

The barn smelled of tar, damp bark and old iron.

The word “hardware” came by itself.

Sofia stopped at the door.

She put her palm on the board.

The board was cold.

Normal.

She almost got angry at this.

What kind of stupidity is it to be angry at the board for being a board.

It was dark in the barn. Light passed through the cracks between the boards in thin gray stripes. Firewood lay along the wall: on the right, dry, as Marek said; on the left is darker, heavier, with moist bark. She took the first log on the right, then the second, the third. The dry wood was lighter than it looked. In the hand it sounded different: not with a voice, of course, but with weight.

Dry - ready to fire now.

Wet - still holds water.

She put several logs in the basket.

On the bottom shelf were thin splinters. She took a handful, then froze.

Too much?

The question was funny.

Beams. Not bread. Not a winter supply. Not the fate of the world. Just a few thin dry wood chips for the stove.

But the hand stopped.

She heard Ivar:

"The earth is not greedy. It just remembers who took it to eat and who took it to hide."

Then she took one again.

And she almost laughed.

If in the morning a person begins to negotiate with a torch, it means that the night really was not in vain.

- No.

Regular.

Uneven.

Light on a dark board.

Previously, crumbs were something to be brushed away for her. Small debris after eating. Now each crumb looked like a trace: the bread was whole, then it became a slice, then it entered the hands, the mouth, the body, the warmth. Nothing has disappeared. Everything has moved on.

Nora collected the crumbs with her palm and poured them into a small bowl by the window.

- For birds? - asked Sofia.

Nora nodded.

- If not for birds, then for something small.

- What if no one comes?

Nora looked at the bowl.

- Then we'll see tomorrow.

Sofia smiled.

Nick approached her and said very quietly:

Nick looked at Ivar at the stove, then at Thomas, who was finishing his slice without the previous protection, then again at Sofia.

- Too much.

Because it was exactly the word she was missing.

About the book

for the reader

"Sampo" opens the cycle “Radiance” not with a museum legend or with a ready-made mythological symbol. The book enters the northern world before the story becomes decoration: the world of home, forest, water, work, scarcity, waiting, and the human need to call the source one's own.

Sampo here is not only a lost object and not only a mythical machine of abundance. This is a question: why does a person strive to own the source, already being inside the world that feeds him?

The book is written as a literary text, but works as a study: through scene, thing, speech, silence and choice, it tests whether an ancient image can again become a living form of understanding.

No spoilers

what the reader will find inside

North house

The house in “Sampo” is not a decoration or a cozy postcard. It is a place where people can gather, stay warm, argue, be silent, eat, listen and bear each other's presence.

Thing and source

The book's focus is not on the hunt for an artifact, but on testing the human desire to obtain abundance without complicity with the order that holds that abundance.

Ivar and Mera

Ivar does not explain the world like a teacher and does not decorate it with mysticism. His presence holds something important: not every depth needs speech, and not every thing becomes yours if you understand it.

Myth before myth

The book does not reconstruct folklore. It enters into the imaginary density of a world where such stories could become necessary because otherwise a person would not retain the law in memory.

Art Research Frame

for funds and partners

“Radiance” is a literary and philosophical artistic and research cycle about how ancient stories preserve figurative instructions for the world. Finnish-Karelian and Kalevala material is the main northern spine of the cycle.

“Sampo” explores the instruction of abundance and participation. This is not an academic study of the epic and not a fantasy setting based on mythological material. The artistic form here becomes a way of checking: what the ancient image preserves, what it warns against, and what human participation it requires.

Sampo is not a question of where the source is. This is the question of why a person wants to own the source while being inside it.

Topics

first research nodes of the book

Abundance

Not as wealth and not as a reward, but as a question of what feeds the world and what a person does with what he receives.

Ownership

The desire to call a source one's own is the point where gratitude can turn into capture.

Participation

The book asks whether it is possible to receive the fruit of the world without agreeing to be part of its living order.

Labor and miracle

The miracle in “Sampo” does not cancel work, cold, body, craft and responsibility. It goes through them.

House

The home becomes a place of moderation: it welcomes people, but does not turn the meeting into a comfortable illusion of security.

North

The north here is neither exotic nor a costume. It is a way to hear in a world where excess quickly becomes dangerous.

For whom

reader entrance

This book may appeal to readers who value slow mythopoetic prose, a northern atmosphere without fantasy clichés, philosophical depth without lecture, and an ancient image that operates through life, the body, work and human choice.

"Sampo" does not require the reader to know "Kalevala". Something else is more important to her: the readiness to enter a world where the fairy tale has not yet become fiction, but the thing still remembers that it was a sign.
