What Radiance is

in brief

Epics, fairy tales and ancient stories of different peoples can be read as different imaginal languages of access to deep knowledge. Each people received its own tools and instructions in a form shaped by its land, language, memory, pain, climate, labour and way of hearing the world.

Radiance is a literary-philosophical, practice-based research cycle about how ancient stories preserve imaginal instructions for the world.

The Finno-Karelian and Kalevala material forms the main northern backbone of the cycle. We try to understand what human experience made these images necessary — and whether artistic form can make this knowledge audible and clearly understandable again.

Artistic research frame

for foundations and partners

Radiance does not prove ancient truth as a thesis. It tests whether artistic form can make it audible and clearly understandable again.

Sampo researches the instruction of abundance and participation.

Song researches the instruction of word, hearing and attunement.

Further books unfold instructions of craft, threshold, trauma, return, birth, forest, measure and responsibility.

This is not a retelling of myth and not decorative mythology. It is an attempt, through the novel, scene, object, speech, silence and human choice, to return working force to the ancient image.

Who this project is for

readers and partners

Readers

The cycle may speak to readers who need slow mythopoetic prose, a northern atmosphere without fantasy cliché, philosophical depth without a lecture, and an ancient image acting through daily life, body, language and choice.

Partners

The project is open to publishing, translation, editorial, residency and cultural collaboration, including public readings, talks and presentations of artistic research through fiction.

What must not be confused

project boundaries

Radiance is not a retelling of mythology, not a fantasy universe and not a spiritual teaching. It is a literary-philosophical, practice-based research cycle in which ancient images are treated as living forms of knowledge.

The project works with myth not as ornament, but as memory: with what helped a human being understand world, measure, labour, word, fear, responsibility and connection with the living order of reality.
