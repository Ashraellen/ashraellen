Author and origin of the work

Ashraellen is the working name of Nikolai Kostyshev, an independent writer and artistic researcher living in Poland.

I spent nearly a decade incarcerated in Belarus. That experience trained the faculty at the centre of this work: sustained observation under pressure. I read extensively and watched, at close range, how fear, hierarchy, language, violence, belief and self-deception can reorganise a human being.

Later, living across Belarus, Kazakhstan, Russia and Poland sharpened the central question: how much of what a person calls reality is direct perception, and how much is interpretation imposed by fear, habit, ideology, bureaucracy, trauma or the surrounding information environment?

The biography is not presented as a request for sympathy. It is the origin of a research instrument: observation from within systems rather than from a protected distance.

Russian is the primary language in which the original meanings, rhythm and inner pressure of the work arise. Other language versions are created as careful adaptations and channels of access to that source.

Flagship literary cycle: MONOLITH

MONOLITH is the principal long-form literary-philosophical project. It is a dystopian trilogy about control, memory and the collapse of systems, structured as three states of social matter: BETON, SLUDGE and GAS.

BETON studies hardened stability, where order becomes a prison. SLUDGE studies viscous deformation, where a person gradually loses form and consents to the environment. GAS moves toward decompression, where former structures can no longer contain meaning.

The series works through scenes, protocols, memory, procedure, fatigue and internal noise. Research takes place inside the fiction rather than above it.

Method of observation

The work begins not with a doctrine to prove, but with an observable mechanism: a moment when language, fear, habit, pain, ideology or digital pressure begins to think in place of the person.

Artistic form is used as a way of knowing. The novel allows a mechanism to unfold across time; satire exposes a defence by changing its scale; essays and short formulas preserve observations at the moment of clarity; public response tests whether a description is recognised beyond the author’s private experience.

The project does not offer a system of help or spiritual instruction. It creates forms of observation.
