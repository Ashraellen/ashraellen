WHISPER

Who is speaking?

WHISPER is a project for a personal AI language prompter. In the intended interaction, a short prompt arrives through an earbud and the person speaks the reply themselves.

The practical aim is simple: help a person understand another speaker and answer in a language they barely know or do not know at all — while remaining the visible, audible and responsible participant in the conversation.

The machine may supply language. The human must not disappear from the conversation.

Project author: Ashraellen. WHISPER is a working name.

01

The problem

Translation can already make a sentence understandable. That does not automatically make a conversation yours.

In a live exchange there is no time to stop, open an app, reconstruct context, write a perfect sentence and then return to the person. Meaning, timing, tone and social presence all matter at once.

WHISPER begins from a more practical question:

What can I naturally say to this person right now?

Its narrow interaction model is:

interlocutor speaks → system interprets bounded context → one short reply is proposed → the user speaks

The project is related to Voiceprint, but not identical to it. Voiceprint asks what must survive when a text passes through language and machines. WHISPER brings that question into a live social encounter.

02

How the project began

The project grew from Ashraellen’s own language needs: speaking Russian fluently, understanding some Polish in familiar situations, yet struggling to formulate a reply. Travel to places with an unfamiliar language widened the question: how do you take part in a conversation when you do not yet have the words?

A preserved discussion from 22 August 2026 already describes short in-ear prompts, five forms of language assistance and the limits of authorship. This records an earlier idea rather than establishing its exact birth date. WHISPER became a standalone project on 4 September.

The first aim is a tool the author can use personally in everyday Polish conversations. The broader direction covers other languages and travel situations. On 6 October, the listening requirement was clarified: within a started session, the system must already be listening when the other person speaks, without separately recording each turn.

03

How assistance should work

Before the exchange, the person sets a bounded situation: language, immediate aim and proficiency. The interlocutor speaks → the system considers that context → proposes a short target-language reply → delivers it through an earbud → the person answers.

A prompt should be natural, brief and manageable to pronounce. One earbud is the preferred scenario so the surroundings remain audible. The device and delivery method still need testing.

An illustrative scenario, not a test result. In a café, the person has already specified that they want a takeaway drink. The other speaker asks a question about the order; the system should suggest a short answer consistent with that intention. If the question’s meaning or the person’s choice is unknown, the system must not confidently invent it.

04

Five assistance contracts

These describe how participation is shared between a person and AI; they are not five implemented application features.

TRANSLATE ME — the human already knows what they want to say; AI transfers it with minimal added authority.

LIVE TRANSCREATION — the human supplies the utterance; AI preserves meaning, intention and style in natural target-language speech.

HELP ME SAY IT — the human supplies the intended meaning; AI helps formulate a natural sentence.

SUGGEST A REPLY — AI proposes a possible answer; the human decides whether it is actually theirs.

WHISPER — bounded live context is used to propose one short immediate response that the human can repeat aloud.

These are not interchangeable features. They represent different degrees of machine authority.

05

Authorship and agency

The project is not primarily asking whether AI can translate. It asks what happens to authorship when language itself is partly supplied by a machine.

A person may still use their own body and voice while the wording has migrated elsewhere.

At what point does linguistic assistance become delegated authorship?

WHISPER therefore treats latency, misunderstanding, trust, ambiguity, dependence and perceived authorship as part of the research — not merely as interface problems.

06

Listening without permanent surveillance

A real conversation cannot depend on pressing a record button after the other person has already begun speaking.

The current requirement is therefore continuous listening only inside an explicitly started, bounded session — for example one taxi ride, one meeting or one service interaction.

continuous inside an explicit session ≠ permanent ambient listening

The system must still learn when not to intervene, whose speech matters, whether a reply is actually needed and how to fail safely when the scene is unclear.

07

What needs to be tested

Usefulness depends on whether prompting helps a person participate. Initial controlled tests should examine:

  • time from the other speaker’s utterance to a usable prompt;
  • fit with the situation and the person’s intention;
  • whether the phrase can be heard, understood and repeated;
  • unwanted interventions, missed assistance and wrong-speaker detection;
  • attention demands, the ability to reject a prompt and perceived authorship.

Polish provides a familiar baseline where partial understanding helps expose mistakes. Testing with a genuinely unfamiliar language comes later. Learning through repeated use of phrases remains a hypothesis.

08

Current status

As of 6 October 2026: the project’s history, principles and five assistance models have been assembled. A personal WHISPER Skill exists as a set of AI behaviour instructions. It does not itself provide a continuous microphone runtime.

A working audio prototype and results from real-world tests have not yet been documented. Continuous listening within a session is a development requirement. Architecture and technical stack have not been approved.

The next step is to define the first version’s behaviour: when to listen or stay silent, how to distinguish voices, deliver a prompt and let the person reject it. Then come a minimal prototype and controlled tests in everyday, low-stakes situations.

No implementation stack is presented here as final. No public claim of proven latency, accuracy or usefulness is being made before real tests exist.

Prototype first. Record failures. Preserve human agency. Then decide what WHISPER actually is.
